# Time and Offline Progression

Version: Draft v1.0

Status: Design Archive

---

# Purpose

Time exists independently of the child.

The universe advances while the application is closed.

---

# Real Time

LUMI uses real elapsed time.

When the child returns, the simulation estimates what happened during the absence.

---

# Offline Simulation

Offline progression should not always simulate every minute.

Instead, multiple abstraction levels are used.

Example

30 minutes

Almost full simulation.

---

6 hours

Simplified simulation.

---

3 days

Aggregated simulation.

---

10 days

Very abstract simulation.

---

After approximately ten days, the world gradually becomes static.

This prevents confusing the child with an overwhelming number of unseen events.

The world waits in a believable state until the child returns.

---

# Why the Ten-Day Limit Exists

Children may forget previous stories.

Freezing the world after extended absence preserves narrative continuity.

The child returns to a familiar world rather than an unrecognizable one.

---

# Seasonal Progress

Even during abstraction:

- weather changes
- crops grow
- festivals may occur
- animals migrate
- families age

Only meaningful changes are preserved.

---

# Historical Compression

Many routine events are summarized into historical outcomes rather than simulated individually.

Example

Instead of simulating every market day:

"The village experienced a successful harvest."

is stored as history.

---

# Design Goal

Time should make the world feel alive without making the child feel lost.
