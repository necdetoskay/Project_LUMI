# Utility Evaluator

Version: Draft v1.0

Status: Design Archive

---

# Purpose

The Utility Evaluator scores possible actions before a decision is made.

It does not determine what is objectively best.

It estimates what appears most desirable to the current character.

---

# Philosophy

Utility is subjective.

The same action has different value for different individuals.

Example

Crossing a dark forest

Explorer:
High utility

Fearful child:
Low utility

Hunter:
Medium utility

Lost traveler:
Very high utility

---

# Evaluation Factors

Each candidate action is evaluated using:

- Need Satisfaction
- Emotional Comfort
- Safety
- Curiosity
- Social Approval
- Relationship Impact
- Resource Cost
- Time Cost
- Moral Alignment
- Personal Preferences
- Memory Associations

---

# Dynamic Weighting

Weights change continuously.

Example

When hungry

Food becomes extremely valuable.

When tired

Rest becomes dominant.

During danger

Safety overrides curiosity.

---

# Emotional Bias

Emotions influence evaluation.

Fear exaggerates risks.

Joy encourages exploration.

Sadness reduces initiative.

Hope increases persistence.

---

# Memory Bias

Past experiences modify utility.

A child bitten by a dog will evaluate unfamiliar dogs differently.

Positive memories may increase utility.

Negative memories may reduce it.

---

# Final Selection

Utility does not force behavior.

The Decision Engine may intentionally select a lower-scoring option if personality supports it.

Example

A brave character may knowingly choose danger.

---

# Design Goal

Characters should value actions according to who they are rather than universal rules.
