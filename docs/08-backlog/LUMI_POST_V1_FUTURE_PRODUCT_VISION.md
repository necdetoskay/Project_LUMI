# LUMI Post-v1 / Future Product Vision

**Status:** Future vision / backlog  
**Scope:** Explicitly OUTSIDE LUMI v1  
**Created:** 2026-08-17

## 1. Purpose

This document preserves product, monetization, retention, and growth ideas for consideration after LUMI v1 is complete. It is intentionally separated from the current delivery scope so that these ideas do not create scope creep or delay v1.

Nothing in this document is a commitment for v1. Future items must be validated with real family usage data before implementation.

## 2. Long-term positioning

LUMI should not compete primarily as an "AI children's story generator." General-purpose AI can already generate one-off children's stories.

The stronger long-term product position is:

> **A personal story world that remembers the child, grows with them, and develops over years.**

The defensible value is therefore not simply story generation. It is continuity, memory, relationships, consequences, a living world, personalization, family connection, and accumulated history.

## 3. Core product thesis

A successful long-term LUMI experience should create three forms of value:

1. **Child attachment** — the child wants to return to their character, friends, places, mysteries, and unfinished events.
2. **Parent value** — the parent sees LUMI as meaningful shared time rather than another disposable screen application.
3. **Accumulated world value** — continued use makes the child's world richer and increasingly difficult to replace with a generic story generator.

The key retention question is not only "Was the story good?" but:

> **Does the child ask to return to their LUMI world later?**

## 4. Future product pillars

### P0 candidate — Long-Term Consequence Engine

Past actions should return naturally in future stories.

Example flow:

`child decision -> durable world memory -> relationship/world change -> future opportunity -> meaningful callback`

Example: a child helps an injured fox in an early adventure. Many stories later, the same fox recognizes the child and helps during a crisis.

The goal is to make memory visible and emotionally meaningful rather than merely storing historical context.

### P0 candidate — Living World / "While you were away"

The world should feel as though it continues to exist between sessions, within safe and bounded simulation rules.

Possible return experience:

- rain changed Crystal Valley;
- an NPC completed a small project;
- a strange shadow appeared over a mountain;
- a friend left a letter;
- a previously discovered place changed slightly.

These events may become natural entry points into future stories.

### P0 candidate — NPC Relationship Engine

NPCs should evolve from static story characters into persistent relationships.

Internally the system may track concepts such as trust, closeness, shared history, gifts, unresolved promises, secrets, and NPC-to-NPC relationships. Child-facing UX should express these naturally rather than as unnecessary numerical RPG statistics.

Example: "Luna trusts you much more now."

### P0 candidate — Parent Companion

Because the parent is the paying customer, LUMI should provide value directly to parents without turning children's stories into lessons.

Potential parent views:

- shared story time;
- adventures completed;
- recurring themes such as curiosity, empathy, courage, cooperation;
- meaningful story moments;
- optional conversation prompts for parent and child.

Example: after a story where the character asks a friend for help, LUMI might suggest that the parent talk about why asking for help can sometimes feel difficult.

### P1 candidate — Parent Invisible Director

Parents may optionally provide themes or current life context that LUMI can translate into subtle story situations.

Example input: "A new sibling has arrived and there is some jealousy."

The story should NOT become a direct lecture. The theme could instead emerge naturally through two creatures learning to share attention, responsibility, or belonging.

This feature requires particularly careful child-safety, privacy, and non-diagnostic design.

### P1 candidate — Family and sibling adventures

Multiple children may have their own characters in the same persistent universe. Their characters can have independent adventures and occasionally meet in shared family adventures.

A parent character could eventually participate as well.

This can increase both family value and long-term retention because the accumulated world becomes a shared family artifact.

### P1 candidate — Age-adaptive LUMI

LUMI should be capable of growing with the child.

Possible progression:

- early years: LUMI tells most of the story;
- emerging reader: more reading participation and simple decisions;
- later: child contributes ideas and dialogue;
- older child: collaborative storytelling and world building.

The world and relationships remain continuous while interaction complexity changes with age.

### P2 candidate — Real Life to LUMI Bridge

With explicit parent control, selected positive real-life events may inspire future fictional experiences.

Example: a parent records that the child learned to ride a bicycle. A future adventure may involve the character learning to ride a flying creature, without unnecessarily reproducing private real-life details.

Privacy, parental control, data minimization, and deletion must be first-class requirements.

### P2 candidate — Personalized physical LUMI books

The child's accumulated adventures could be transformed into a high-quality physical keepsake.

Potential product:

**My LUMI Adventure — 2027**

It could contain the character, important NPCs, world map, major adventures, illustrations, milestones, and selected memorable moments.

This provides both an additional revenue stream and a tangible representation of the value accumulated inside LUMI.

### Long-term vision — LUMI Legacy

A child may begin at age five and retain the same evolving world for years.

The product could preserve eras such as:

- First Adventure
- Discovery of Crystal Valley
- Luna Returns
- The Great Expedition

Over time, LUMI becomes a record of the child's imaginative development rather than merely a collection of generated stories.

This accumulated history may become one of LUMI's strongest long-term product moats.

## 5. Monetization hypotheses

Pricing must be validated rather than assumed. An initial hypothesis for future testing is:

### Free

- one child;
- one character;
- limited stories;
- limited image generation;
- enough persistent-world functionality to experience the product's differentiation.

### Family

Initial hypothesis: approximately **EUR 7.99–9.99/month**, with a discounted annual plan around **EUR 69–89/year**.

Possible benefits:

- multiple children/characters;
- richer persistent world;
- increased story allowance;
- premium visual generation;
- audio narration;
- deeper personalization and history;
- advanced parent features.

These are test hypotheses, not committed prices or packaging.

## 6. Commercial validation gates

Before major post-v1 investment, validate three hypotheses separately.

### Hypothesis A — Child attachment

Does the child voluntarily want to return to their character/world?

### Hypothesis B — Parent-perceived differentiation

Does the parent see meaningful value beyond what a generic AI chatbot/story generator provides?

### Hypothesis C — Willingness to pay

Will families actually pay a recurring subscription at the target price range?

A and B can be true while C is false. Therefore product love must not be confused with monetization validation.

Important metrics should eventually include first-story completion, next-day return, 7/30-day family retention, repeated world/NPC engagement, parent activation, free-to-paid conversion, cancellation reasons, and long-term cohort retention.

## 7. Growth thesis — Instagram-first content system

Instagram is a strong candidate for early organic growth because LUMI naturally produces visually interesting characters, locations, maps, story moments, and emotional callbacks.

### Separate English and Turkish presence

The initial hypothesis is to maintain separate English and Turkish accounts rather than mixing both languages in one feed.

English should target the broader international market. Turkish can act as a smaller, valuable learning and validation market.

Localization should adapt tone and cultural context where appropriate rather than mechanically translating every post.

### Content principles

The account should be something parents want to follow, not a feed of product advertisements.

Suggested content families:

1. **Curiosity** — provocative questions and mysteries.
2. **World** — unusual characters, regions, maps, objects, and micro-stories.
3. **Emotion** — callbacks demonstrating that the world remembers.
4. **Parent value** — shared storytelling, conversation, imagination, and family time.
5. **Participation** — audience votes such as "What happens next?"

A majority of posts should provide entertainment, wonder, usefulness, or participation rather than direct sales messaging.

### Example campaign concept

Opening:

> "She created this dragon 47 days ago."

Later:

> "Tonight, he came back. He remembered her."

End:

> **LUMI — Stories that grow with them.**

The purpose is to demonstrate the product difference emotionally without explaining the underlying AI architecture.

## 8. Product-led growth

LUMI may eventually create safe, parent-controlled shareable artifacts such as:

- My LUMI Character;
- My World;
- My Adventure Map;
- Our 10th Adventure;
- memorable story cards.

These should never expose child-private information by default. Sharing must be parent-controlled and privacy-preserving.

A subtle "Created with LUMI" attribution can turn genuine family creations into organic discovery channels.

## 9. Future Content Engine

A later Growth & Content Engine could transform suitable LUMI-generated material into social-media candidates:

`LUMI world/story event -> safe content candidate -> visual/video composition -> EN/TR localization -> human approval -> publication`

Human approval should remain mandatory before publication. Real child data should never automatically become public content.

This system could provide a sustainable content pipeline without requiring the team to invent a new campaign from scratch every day.

## 10. Marketing language

Avoid leading with implementation terminology such as:

- semantic memory;
- context builder;
- persistent state engine;
- world simulation architecture;
- LLM orchestration.

Translate technology into visible human outcomes.

Instead of:

> "LUMI uses persistent semantic memory."

Prefer:

> "Three weeks ago your child rescued a fox. LUMI remembered."

The technology is the mechanism; the remembered relationship is the product.

## 12. Explicit v1 boundary

**None of the features in this document should automatically enter the LUMI v1 implementation backlog.**

The current objective remains completing and validating v1. This document exists so valuable future ideas are not forgotten while protecting the current release from scope expansion.

After v1 reaches sufficient stability, this document should be reviewed alongside real retention, usage, parent feedback, operational cost, and willingness-to-pay data. Only then should candidates be promoted into implementation issues.

## 13. North-star direction

The long-term evolution can be summarized as:

**From:** personalized AI-generated children's stories.

**Toward:** a safe personal story world that remembers the child, develops meaningful relationships and consequences, grows with the family, and can preserve years of imaginative history.
